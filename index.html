How it works

The tank solves the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on a staggered grid. Each step applies your fans, carries the velocity along itself (advection), spreads it by viscosity, then solves for the pressure that makes the flow obey ∇·u = q: zero everywhere except at sources and sinks.

Elements

  • Open is plain fluid. Painting it also erases anything else.
  • Wall is solid and no-slip. Beads bounce off it.
  • Goal is fluid that turns any bead touching it green for good.
  • Inflow holds a fixed velocity and releases beads in proportion to its speed. Set its direction with the dial before painting, or select it afterward and rotate it.

Shaping the flow

  • Velocity painted on open fluid acts as a fan: during a run the fluid there is pulled toward that velocity, while the rest of the tank responds through pressure and viscosity.
  • Select a region to rotate or scale its vectors. Drag the knob on the ring to rotate, or scroll over the tank to scale (Shift + scroll rotates).
  • Comb paints velocity along the direction you drag.
  • Sources and sinks add or remove fluid. Sinks also swallow beads, at the same rate they drain fluid.
  • Curls stamp a vortex into the fan field, so they keep stirring for the whole run.

Reading the tank

Arrows show velocity, colored slate to amber by speed. Rose tint is counterclockwise spin and blue is clockwise. Amber glow is a source and violet is a sink. With open edges, fluid and beads can leave the tank; with walls, an inflow needs a sink to make room.

Keys

1-4 elements, 5-0 modifiers, [ ] brush size, Space simulate or stop, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Esc deselect.

Files

Save downloads two files: a map PNG with one pixel per cell (black open, gray #808080 wall, white goal, cyan #00FFFF inflow) and a .flow.json with the velocity and divergence fields and run settings. You can draw maps in any paint program with those four colors and load them.
